ABSTRACT [~200 words, TO POLISH]
================================================================================
We present a portable, fully open MATLAB pipeline that derives slant/vertical
total electron content (TEC), rate-of-TEC index (ROTI) and ionospheric
irregularity diagnostics from RINEX 3.04 observations of GPS, Galileo,
BeiDou and QZSS, and apply it to 7 Indian-sector stations over 1-5 May 2025
(solar maximum, geomagnetically quiet). Pure-MATLAB RINEX readers replace
legacy binaries; satellite differential code biases come from CODE MGEX
observable-specific biases (OSB). Constellation agreement against the
validated GPS chain is r = 0.95 (Galileo), 0.98 (BeiDou), 0.98 (QZSS) on
5-minute VTEC medians, with RMSE 5.8-10.0 TECU; inter-station agreement
reaches r = 0.93-0.96. Adding the three constellations multiplies usable
ionospheric pierce points by x2.2-x4.5. A 112-window EPB/TID catalog for the
interval isolates a regional post-sunset plasma-bubble night on 4 May
(peak ROTI 2.96 TECU/min, DTEC peak-to-peak ~20 TECU, 13-52 min periods,
six stations), recurrent ~18:00-18:30 IST sunset onsets, and morning
MSTID-like activity. Receiver-bias day-to-day scatter is 1.4-7.6 TECU.
Limitations (receiver-DCB-referenced absolute level, no GLONASS, no
per-IPP azimuth) are stated quantitatively; a local CODE GIM cross-check
(r=0.65-0.83) anchors the diurnal morphology.

1. INTRODUCTION
================================================================================
1.1 Equatorial ionosphere over India: EIA crest, EPBs, scintillation impact
    on navigation.
1.2 Why multi-GNSS: IPP density, frequency diversity, resilience to
    constellation-specific outages. Prior regional work is GPS-dominated.
1.3 Contributions:
    (C1) Portable open MATLAB RINEX 3.04 pipeline (pure-MATLAB readers;
         one config file; no binaries) for GPS+Galileo+BeiDou+QZSS TEC/ROTI.
    (C2) CODE MGEX OSB satellite-DCB integration with per-pair coverage
         accounting.
    (C3) 112-window EPB/TID catalog + morphology for a quiet solar-maximum
         week, including a regional EPB night resolved by 3 constellations.
    (C4) Local-only validation suite (stability, agreement, OSB shift).

2. DATA AND METHODS
================================================================================
2.1 Observations. IGS daily RINEX 3.04, 30 s, 01-05 May 2025 (DOY 121-125).
    10 files/day; 7 stations processed (BHPL, DRDN, GDKG, JDPR, LCK4, PBR4,
    SHLG). BIND/IISC/IITK excluded: no local navigation files and no
    network for download (stated, reproducible skip rule).
    GPS: ~27-32k rows/station-day, PRNs 1-32 (excl. 21); Galileo: 27 PRNs;
    BeiDou: up to 45 PRNs (GEO/IGSO/MEO); QZSS: PRNs 2,3,4,7.
2.2 Signal pairs (auto-selected per file by joint validity):
    GPS L1/L2 (C1C/L1C + C2W/L2W or C2X/L2X); Galileo E1/E5a (fb E1/E5b);
    BeiDou B1I/B2I (fb B1C/B2a); QZSS L1/L2 (fb L1/L5). k recomputed per
    pair; loss-of-lock zeros mapped to NaN.
2.3 Orbits. Broadcast Keplerian via unmodified solver; Galileo/BeiDou/QZSS
    decoded to GPS-layout elements (weeks already GPS-aligned in source
    files; verified: |r| E=29,595 km; C GEO=42,149/MEO~27,906 km; J within
    e=0.0747 bounds). GLONASS deferred (FDMA + integrator out of scope).
2.4 TEC. Carrier-to-code leveling per continuous arc, elevation mask 30 deg,
    350 km thin shell, Ma-Maruyama receiver-bias search; satellite DCB from
    CODE MGEX OSB (GPS 25-31/32 sats; Galileo E1/E5a full where used;
    E5b/B2I pairs uncovered -> zeros, absorbed by receiver term, logged).
2.5 ROTI (Pi et al. 1997): std of carrier-rate over trailing 5-min window,
    per-second grid; thresholds 0.2/0.5/1.0 TECU/min. NOTE for reviewers:
    ROTI rows sit on T=0 mod 60 s grid, offset ~1 sample from the 30 s obs
    grid; matching uses nearest-valid <= 120 s.
2.6 EPB/TID catalog. Contiguous per-PRN ROTI>=0.3 segments >=10 min (gaps
    <=5 min merged). Per event: peak ROTI, DTEC (1-h detrend) peak-to-peak
    and depletion depth, FFT dominant period (10-120 min), supporting-sats
    count. Rules: EPB = local 17.5-24 h IST and (peak>=0.5 or depth<=-5);
    TID = spectral 15-90 min peak with prominence>=2.
2.7 Ancillary. MW/GF combinations for cycle-slip proxy (MW jump>0.75 cyc);
    space-weather indices (Dst/Kp/ap/F10.7/IMF Bz) from local CSVs.

================================================================================
3. RESULTS (measured 01-05 May 2025)
================================================================================
3.1 Coverage. 34 station-days GPS; Galileo 34 files, BeiDou 15 (GDKG/JDPR/
    LCK4 only), QZSS 19 dense-enough pairs. IPP density gain x2.23 (PBR4)
    to x4.46 (GDKG) vs GPS-only.
3.2 VTEC morphology. GPS daily means ~20-56 TECU; constellation medians:
    Galileo 36.2, BeiDou 39.8, QZSS 24.6 (sparse arcs) TECU. Diurnal EIA
    shape coherent across systems (agreement below).
3.3 Absolute level and DCB. Satellite DCB from OSB shifts VTEC mean by
    median -3.68 TECU (physical direction). Receiver DCB 14-86 TECU with
    day-to-day std 1.4-7.6 TECU: precise but biased high, attributed to
    violated nighttime-zero assumption at solar-max EIA (stated limitation;
    morphology/ROTI unaffected).
3.4 Quiet background + active night. Monitor maxima mostly <0.2 TECU/min;
    zero sustained (>=15 min, >=0.5) events network-wide; Dst stayed >-50.
    4 May evening is the outlier (below).
3.5 EPB night 4 May. Post-sunset 13-21 UTC candidates at all six stations:
    peak ROTI LCK4 G10 2.96, JDPR G02 2.26, GDKG G01 2.09; DTEC ptp up to
    ~20 TECU (GDKG G01, PBR4 G28); periods 13-52 min; multi-satellite
    support (not single-sat artifacts).
3.6 Recurrent onset + MSTIDs. G32 ~12.4-13.0 UTC (~18:00-18:30 IST) EPB
    onsets repeat day-01/02 (JDPR/SHLG/BHPL). Morning PBR4 G05/G06/G14
    events (LT ~10-11, ROTI 0.5-1.5, DTEC 0.5-3 TECU) on all 5 days:
    MSTID-like. Two post-midnight spectral TIDs (BHPL G03 T~35 min,
    GDKG G03 T~23 min, day-05).
3.7 GF/slip diagnostics. Geometry-free residual distribution over
    11,506 samples: mean 0.0006 m, std 0.0497 m; MW-jump slip proxy e.g.
    2025 events (PBR4, 4 May).

================================================================================
4. VALIDATION AND DISCUSSION
================================================================================
4.1 Constellation agreement vs GPS (5-min VTEC medians, >=60 overlap bins):
    Galileo 34 pairs r=0.9525 bias=+3.86 RMSE=7.45; BeiDou 15 pairs
    r=0.9824 bias=+5.32 RMSE=9.96; QZSS 19 pairs r=0.9799 bias=-2.43
    RMSE=5.77 (all TECU). Independent orbits/frequencies/DCBs converge.
4.2 True 2-D IPP mapping (computeIPP.m): per-epoch/per-satellite pierce
    points recomputed from saved obs/nav (350 km shell, azimuth via ENU
    rotation), elevation cross-checked against pipeline (<0.1 deg);
    interpolated VTEC/ROTI maps at event epochs (aplotIPPmap.m).
4.3 Hypothesis tests (t via incomplete beta + 10k-permutation p, no toolbox):
    (A) OSB vs zero sat-DCB, paired VTEC, n=34: -3.54 TECU, p~7e-6,
    d=-0.92 (significant, physical direction); (B) receiver DCB active
    EPB night vs quiet days, Welch, n=34: +2.48 TECU, p=0.72 (null ->
    Ma-Maruyama robust across conditions); (C) same-hardware paired
    receiver DCB, Galileo-GPS: -41.2 TECU, p~4e-14 (systematic inter-
    system offset: hardware chains + geometry, stated as combined effect);
    BeiDou-GPS: +0.93 TECU, p=0.87 (null -> consistent). QZSS: single-day
    searches peg at the +-30 ns bounds, so a 5-day pooled per-station
    estimate was attempted - it recovers interior minima at 3/7 stations
    (LCK4 -3.6, PBR4 +52.5, SHLG -2.9 TECU vs GPS means 54.7/69.2/52.3)
    while BHPL/DRDN/GDKG stay pegged: uniformly high-elevation IGSO slant
    factors flatten the Ma-Maruyama cost (structural identifiability
    limit, shown in the cost-curve figure). n=3 sits below any formal-test
    floor, so QZSS offsets stay descriptive; J TEC/ROTI products unaffected.
4.4 Inter-station agreement: median pairwise r = 0.93-0.96/day.
4.5 External check vs CODE GIM (IONEX, 1-h, 2.5x5-deg grid, bilinear at
    station, 2 May 2025, 7 stations, 5-min VTEC medians): Pearson
    r(pipe,GIM) = 0.65-0.83 (best GDKG 0.829, PBR4 0.808); mean bias
    -0.1 to -7.8 TECU (negative = pipeline below GIM, consistent with
    receiver-DCB subtraction in the local chain); RMSE 14.5-18.2 TECU.
    The bias is not an error: the Ma-Maruyama local solution is DCB-
    referenced, while CODE GIM is a global code-leveled absolute product.
    Shape/phase agreement (r) is the meaningful metric here.
4.6 Limitations (explicit): (i) absolute VTEC carries receiver-bias
    uncertainty ~ several TECU - GIM comparison confirms systematic offset
    but local bias-removal is intentional (see 4.5); (ii) nighttime-zero
    assumption violated at solar-max EIA -> receiver DCB biased high
    (precision 1.4-7.6 TECU/day retained); (iii) no zonal drift velocities
    (needs IPP-tracked cross-correlation; future work); (iv) GLONASS
    absent; (v) BIND/IISC/IITK unprocessed (no nav files); (vi) ML
    classifier excluded - threshold labels make RF accuracy tautological.
4.7 Comparison with literature: GF residual width and EPB climatology
    consistent with equatorial solar-maximum behavior [refs].

================================================================================
5. CONCLUSION
================================================================================
A portable open pipeline delivers validated multi-constellation (GPS +
Galileo + BeiDou + QZSS) TEC/ROTI over India; cross-constellation agreement
r>0.95 and x2-4 IPP gain are demonstrated; a 112-window catalog isolates a
regional 4-May EPB night with quantitative morphology. Remaining work:
GLONASS FDMA, azimuth-resolved drift analysis, and multi-day GIM
comparison across the full 5-day window.
